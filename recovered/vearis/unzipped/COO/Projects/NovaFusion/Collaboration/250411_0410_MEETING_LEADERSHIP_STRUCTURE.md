## Key Decision Framework

**Decision 1: Phased Implementation Approach**
- **Options**: 
  - Option A: 3-phase approach (Foundation, Integration, Evolution)
  - Option B: 2-phase approach (Foundation+Integration, Evolution)
  - Option C: 4-phase approach (separate Integration into two phases)
- **Recommendation**: Option A - 3-phase approach as outlined in collaborative documents
- **Rationale**: Creates clear milestones while maintaining momentum; aligns with existing technical documents

**Decision 2: Initial Focus Areas**
- **Options**:
  - Option A: Equal focus across all components
  - Option B: Primary focus on Observable Infrastructure and prototypes
  - Option C: Primary focus on one component (e.g., FUSION BUS)
- **Recommendation**: Option B - Focus on Observable Infrastructure and prototypes
- **Rationale**: Creates foundation for all future work while delivering immediate value

### 2. Cross-Team Structure Decisions

**Decision 3: Team Organization Model**
- **Options**:
  - Option A: Matrix organization with home teams + cross-functional teams
  - Option B: Dedicated NOVA FUSION teams pulled from existing teams
  - Option C: Virtual teams with part-time allocation from home teams
- **Recommendation**: Option A - Matrix organization
- **Rationale**: Maintains operational continuity while enabling cross-team collaboration

**Decision 4: Joint Team Structure**
- **Options**:
  - Option A: 5 teams as proposed in Cosmos' synthesis
  - Option B: 3 teams aligned with FUSION components
  - Option C: 2 teams (technical foundation + integration)
- **Recommendation**: Option A - 5 teams as proposed
- **Rationale**: Provides focused effort on key synergy areas while maintaining manageable team sizes

### 3. Technical Standards Decisions

**Decision 5: Core Integration Mechanism**
- **Options**:
  - Option A: Redis-based message bus with ZeroPoint extensions

  - Option C: New unified protocol developed specifically for FUSION

**Decision 6: Interface Contract Approach**

  - Option B: Distributed interface definitions with cross-team review process
  - Option C: Component-based interfaces owned by implementing teams

## Decision Facilitation Approach

For each decision point, I propose the following facilitation approach:

1. **Frame the Decision** (1 minute)
   - Clear statement of the decision to be made
   - Presentation of options

2. **Team Input** (3 minutes)
   - Brief input from each team leader
   - Focus on critical considerations

3. **Discussion** (3 minutes)
   - Moderated discussion of trade-offs
   - Clarifying questions

4. **Decision** (1 minute)
   - Explicit decision by consensus or executive decision
   - Recording of decision and rationale

This approach ensures we can make all necessary decisions within the meeting timeframe while ensuring all perspectives are considered.

## Post-Meeting Follow-Through

To ensure effective implementation of meeting decisions, I propose:

1. **Decision Documentation**
   - All decisions documented in meeting minutes
   - Rationale and implications captured
   - Distribution to all stakeholders within 24 hours

2. **Action Item Tracking**
   - All action items entered into tracking system
   - Clear ownership and deadlines
   - Regular status reporting

3. **Communication Cascade**
   - Leadership briefing of their teams within 48 hours
   - Consistent messaging using approved communication materials
   - Clear context on how decisions impact each team

4. **Weekly Integration Sync**
   - 30-minute weekly sync across all joint teams
   - Focus on cross-team dependencies and blockers
   - Quick decision-making on integration issues

## Leadership Meeting Success Criteria

The leadership meeting will be successful if we achieve:

1. **Decision Completion**: All key decisions made with clear rationale
2. **Team Alignment**: Shared understanding and commitment to approach
3. **Resource Clarity**: Clear allocation of people and infrastructure
4. **Action Clarity**: Specific next steps with ownership and timing
5. **Communication Alignment**: Shared messaging about the vision and approach
